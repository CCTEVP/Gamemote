**One-sentence pitch:** Drive a real gadget from your iPhone or iPad with a squishy clay
gamepad, then teach it tricks by snapping blocks into routines it can perform on its own.

## Core loop
- Connect the gadget and drive it in **Live** mode with the gamepad to discover what it can do.
- Switch to the **Routine builder** and snap blocks into a routine; tap a block to tweak its value.
- **Run** it to watch from the app, or **Transfer** it so the gadget performs it alone; fix and run again.
- **Save** the routine and pick it up next time.

## Player goal & fail state — what "working" looks like
- A session feels complete when the player built a routine and watched the gadget do what they meant.
- Nothing ends in failure: if the gadget stops responding or disconnects, it goes still within
  0.5 s and the app reconnects on its own. A misbehaving routine is fixed by editing, and the
  app never crashes.

## MVP — what must exist to be the game
1. **Home and connect**: find the gadget, connect, then choose **Live** or **Routine builder**
   (two separate modes).
2. **Live mode**: the clay gamepad (stick, four buttons, STOP), set up from the gadget's own
   channels and actions; multitouch and haptic taps.
3. **Routine builder**: Set, Trigger, Wait and Repeat blocks; snap to add, tap to edit, drag
   back to delete; Run Live, Transfer and Stop; the running block glows.
4. **Saved routines**: a named library that survives closing the app (open, rename, delete).
5. **Soft tactile sounds** for press, snap, run and stop, matching the clay feel.
6. **Kid-friendly basics**: big touch targets, picture-first labels, and STOP reachable at
   all times in both modes.
7. **Peek**: drag empty table space to see the 3D shapes; the view springs back to top-down.

## Out of scope — not building this
- No accounts, cloud sync or sharing.
- One gadget at a time.
- No conditions, sensors, variables or functions in blocks.
- No Android, and no App Store release (free Apple account).
- No layout editor for the gamepad.
- No firmware for a specific board yet; the Mac simulator stands in until one is chosen.

## Build order
1. **Split the table prototype into Home, Live and Routine builder.** First, because it proves
   the two-mode flow and whether the builder fits a small iPhone screen in landscape.
2. **Saved routines library** (data format and storage).
3. **Kid-friendly pass**: target sizes, picture-first labels, STOP everywhere.
4. **Sounds.**
5. **Polish**: snap and run animations, plain-language connection messages.
